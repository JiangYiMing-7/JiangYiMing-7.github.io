About

I am a final-year undergraduate in Artificial Intelligence at Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications (BUPT), graduating in 2027. Since September 2026 I have been a visiting research student at NTU Singapore, working with Asst. Prof. Bryan Lim.

My research is on trustworthy LLMs and agents in multi-step interaction: how safety risk accumulates across turns, how to attribute a failure to the steps that caused it, and whether internal representations point to interventions that really improve downstream behaviour. My interest in multimodal models grew out of earlier work on multimodal sentiment analysis and depression assessment, and I now study whether latent reasoning steps in vision-language models are actually used.

I am applying to PhD programs starting in Fall 2027.

Current research

Turn-level credit assignment for LLM safety alignment under multi-turn jailbreaks

In progressFirst author, NTU, supervised by Asst. Prof. Bryan Lim; targeting ACL 2027

Which turns of a multi-turn jailbreak deserve safety supervision? I replace a selected model response with a safe one and let the attacker adapt, to measure how much each turn drives later harm. A hidden-state turn selector did not consistently beat a last-turn baseline, so I am now weighting turns for on-policy self-distillation by measured intervention effect rather than representational sensitivity.

Latent-space reasoning in multimodal LLMs

In progressIndependent project; targeting ICML 2027

A frozen Qwen3-VL reasons through a few iterative hidden-state steps instead of a long chain of thought, guided by two lightweight directors that re-attend to the image and probe the model’s own knowledge. Shuffle, removal and matched static-prompt controls test whether the latent steps are causally used. Across three seeds, latent steps become necessary once the answer is cut off from the image, but the advantage of adaptive routing disappears against tuned baselines, so what the latent steps carry is still an open question.
